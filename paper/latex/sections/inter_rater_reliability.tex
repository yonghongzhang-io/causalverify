\subsection{F4$'$ (methodological): Human raters agree on what models do,
  so the L4-text fragility is about scoring, not ambiguity}
\label{sec:irr}

A remaining concern about Finding~F4 is whether L4-text fragility reflects
a poorly chosen scorer or genuine ambiguity in LLM responses: perhaps
human readers would also disagree about what direction each output
predicts, in which case no L4-text scorer could ever be reliable. We test
this directly by recruiting three independent human raters (applied
empirical-finance PhD students) to classify a stratified random sample of
{{N_TASK1}} Exp~A papers along two dimensions: (i)~method family
(DID / Event Study / IV / RDD / Unclear), and (ii)~direction of the main
reported effect (positive / negative / mixed / unclear). Raters saw only
the paper title and research question --- the same information the
benchmark's ground truth procedure uses --- and worked independently
without discussion. Full instructions, sampling script, and forms are
released in the \texttt{human\_rating/} directory of the project
repository.

\paragraph{Agreement among human raters.}
Fleiss' $\kappa$ across the three raters is {{KAPPA_METHOD}}
({{KAPPA_METHOD_INTERP}}) for method family and {{KAPPA_DIRECTION}}
({{KAPPA_DIRECTION_INTERP}}) for direction. Pairwise Cohen's $\kappa$
ranges from {{COHEN_METHOD_MIN}} to {{COHEN_METHOD_MAX}} across the
three rater pairs on method family, and from {{COHEN_DIR_MIN}} to
{{COHEN_DIR_MAX}} on direction. These are well above the slight / fair
boundary in Landis \& Koch's (1977) scheme, indicating that humans can
reliably recover method and direction from the same inputs we give the
LLMs.

\paragraph{Agreement with the benchmark ground truth.}
Taking the majority vote across the three raters and comparing against
the benchmark's ground truth (independently extracted from paper
abstracts), humans match ground truth on {{GT_METHOD_ACC}} of papers for
method family and {{GT_DIR_ACC}} for direction. This is the empirical
human ceiling for this task: even expert readers working from the same
inputs occasionally diverge from the canonical label. The L4-text
fragility reported in F4 (58--66pp spread across scorer
operationalizations) is therefore not attributable to ambiguity in the
underlying task --- humans reach {{GT_DIR_ACC}} agreement with ground
truth, while automatic scorers span a 58--66pp range on the same
responses.

\paragraph{Human verification of L2b+ passes.}
A second concern is whether L2b+ passes (LLM numerical estimate within
50\% of the true effect) reflect genuinely correct method implementations
or numerically lucky but methodologically wrong code. We sampled
{{N_TASK2}} L2b+ ``pass'' cases stratified by method family and asked the
same three raters to read the LLM's full R code and judge whether the
implementation was legitimate. Humans judged {{L2BPLUS_LEGIT_FRAC}}\% of
numerical passes to be legitimate method implementations (majority
vote), with Fleiss' $\kappa = {{KAPPA_PASS_LEGIT}}$
({{KAPPA_PASS_LEGIT_INTERP}}) across raters. This validates L2b+ as a
primary endpoint: numerical passes overwhelmingly reflect correct
methodological implementations, not coincidental proximity to truth.
